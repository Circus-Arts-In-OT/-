About the Program

This website highlights how circus arts can be used within occupational therapy to create engaging therapeutic experiences. Activities such as scarf juggling, balance games, creative movement, performance-based art, and group challenges can be adapted to meet the needs of children, older adults, and individuals with diverse abilities.

Areas Addressed

Motor Skills

Supports coordination, balance, reaching, hand-eye coordination, bilateral coordination, and endurance.

Cognition

Encourages attention, sequencing, problem-solving, memory, following directions, and motor planning.

Social Participation

Promotes peer interaction, turn-taking, teamwork, communication, and shared group experiences.

Confidence & Engagement

Builds self-expression, motivation, creativity, confidence, and willingness to participate in new activities.

Circus-Based Activity Ideas

Scarf Juggling

  • Toss and catch one scarf.
  • Progress to two scarves.
  • Add clapping, reaching, or partner tosses.

Bean Bag Toss

  • Toss bean bags into buckets or hoops.
  • Change distance to grade difficulty.
  • Use color matching or point values.

Balloon Balance

  • Keep balloons in the air using hands or pool noodles.
  • Work individually or in groups.
  • Add music or timed challenges.

Showtime Art Board

  • Create circus performer-themed artwork.
  • Share a word, story, or role at the end.
  • Display completed work as a group project.

Grading & Adaptations

Grade Down

Use larger or lighter materials, decrease distance, allow seated participation, provide visual demonstrations, reduce steps, or offer hand-over-hand assistance as appropriate.

Grade Up

Add more steps, increase distance, include partner work, use timed challenges, add sequencing patterns, or combine movement with memory tasks.

Inclusive Participation

Activities can be completed seated, standing, individually, with a partner, or as a group to support different mobility, sensory, cognitive, and social needs.
